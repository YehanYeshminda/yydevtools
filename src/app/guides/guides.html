Why these guides exist

A tool can show you an answer without ever telling you what it means. You can decode a JWT and still not know that decoding proves nothing about who issued it. You can Base64-encode a password and come away believing you protected it. You can write a cron expression for the thirteenth of the month, land on a schedule that also fires every Friday, and not find out for weeks. Every one of those is a mistake made by someone who used the right tool correctly and had the wrong model in their head.

These guides are for the model. Each one takes a single idea that developers meet constantly and rarely have explained — what is actually inside a token, why encoding and encryption are different things, where the size of an image really comes from — and walks through it in plain language, with the specific misunderstandings that cause real bugs called out where they belong.

They are written to be read on their own rather than as documentation for a tool. Where trying something makes the idea concrete, a guide links to the tool that does it, and the tool pages link back. Nothing here is generated or reworded from somewhere else: each guide is written from scratch, and is revised when something changes or an explanation turns out not to land.

What is coming

The set grows as the tools do. Subjects being written next include certificates and the chain of trust, what actually happens when a browser makes an HTTPS connection, the difference between hashing a password and encrypting it, and how image formats decide what to throw away. If there is something you have always half-understood and would like written up properly, say so — several of these started as exactly that kind of question.
